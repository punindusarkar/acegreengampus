"The Indian Almond (Terminalia catappa), also known as the Tropical Almond or Sea Almond, is a large deciduous tree belonging to the Combretaceae family. Native to tropical Asia, Africa, and Australia, this tree thrives in warm, coastal, and tropical regions due to its high salt tolerance. It has broad, leathery leaves that turn red or yellow before falling, creating a stunning seasonal display. The tree produces greenish-white flowers that develop into drupe-like fruits, inside which the almond-like edible seeds are found. These seeds are rich in healthy fats and proteins. The leaves, bark, and seeds have been widely used in traditional medicine, particularly for digestive health, skin conditions, and antimicrobial benefits. The leaves also contain antioxidants and are often used in herbal teas and aquariums to improve water quality and fish health. Besides its medicinal uses, the Indian Almond tree plays an essential environmental role by absorbing carbon dioxide and releasing oxygen, contributing to a cleaner atmosphere. It is commonly grown for shade, ornamental landscaping, and erosion control in tropical and coastal areas. With its nutritional, medicinal, and environmental benefits, the Indian Almond is a valuable tree in both urban and natural ecosystems."
