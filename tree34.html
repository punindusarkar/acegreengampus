Scientific Name Pandanus tectorius
Common Name Tahitian Screwpine, Hala Tree, Pandanus Palm
Family Pandanaceae
Type Small tree or shrub
Leaf Type Long, narrow, sword-shaped leaves with spiny edges, arranged in a spiral pattern
Flower Color Creamy white (male flowers) and inconspicuous (female flowers)

Water Requirements Low to moderate; drought-tolerant but prefers moist, well-drained soil

"Pandanus tectorius, commonly known as Screwpine or Pandan, is a tropical tree or shrub belonging to the Pandanaceae family. Native to Southeast Asia, Pacific Islands, and the Indian Ocean, it is widely cultivated in tropical regions for its versatile uses. The plant grows in moist, coastal, and tidal areas, tolerating saltwater, making it ideal for shoreline stabilization. The long, narrow, strap-like leaves of the Screwpine grow in a spiral arrangement, which gives the tree its name. The plant produces small greenish-yellow flowers and later develops large, oval fruits that resemble pineapples. The fruit is edible and consumed in various traditional dishes across Southeast Asia and the Pacific Islands. The leaves of the plant are highly valued in culinary applications, where they are used to flavor food, especially in sweets and rice dishes. Beyond its culinary value, Pandanus tectorius has medicinal uses, such as treating skin infections, coughs, and digestive problems. The plant’s wood is used for crafting mats, rafts, and baskets. With its tolerance to saltwater, it plays an essential role in coastal management, stabilizing soil and preventing erosion. It also acts as a carbon dioxide absorber, contributing to air quality."
