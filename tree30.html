"Annona squamosa, commonly known as Sugar Apple, Sweetsop, or Custard Apple, is a deciduous shrub or small tree belonging to the Annonaceae family. It is native to tropical America and the West Indies but is widely cultivated in India, Southeast Asia, and Africa for its sweet, segmented fruit. The tree has simple, lanceolate green leaves and produces pale green to yellow fragrant flowers. The fruit is round, green, and bumpy, with a soft, creamy, and sweet pulp, often compared to custard. This tree thrives in warm, humid climates, requiring moderate watering and well-drained soil. It grows up to 3–8 meters and begins fruiting within 3–4 years. The sugar apple is nutrient-rich, containing Vitamin C, B6, iron, and fiber, which support digestion, immunity, and energy production. In traditional medicine, the tree is valued for its antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, and analgesic properties. It is used to treat diabetes, dysentery, and skin infections. However, the seeds are toxic if consumed and are often used in insect repellents. Environmentally, Annona squamosa contributes to carbon dioxide absorption and oxygen release, improving air quality. Due to its nutritional, medicinal, and economic importance, this tree is widely cultivated and appreciated worldwide."
